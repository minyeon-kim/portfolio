<TLDR>
- **Problem:** Gemini can pull Gmail, Calendar and Photos into an answer, but people can't see where a detail came from, and turning it off means digging through two settings pages.
- **My role:** Research with one teammate, then solo from problem definition to a coded prototype and usability test.
- **Outcome:** A working prototype that shows the source under the answer, turns it off in place, and lets people check that a deletion worked. Usability test in progress.
</TLDR>

<CaseSection id="overview" label="01 / Overview" title="Connecting took one tap. | Cleaning up took a trip to settings.">

In January 2026, Google launched [Personal Intelligence](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/personal-intelligence/). Turn it on, and Gemini reads your Gmail, Calendar, Photos, YouTube and Search history to personalize answers, even when you don't ask it to. A writer at [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/gemini-cant-forget-my-ex/) asked for a date-night restaurant and got a dish recommendation, plus a reminder that they'd been there with Sarah, an ex, last spring.

The convenience is real. The problem is control. The answer doesn't say which app a memory came from, and the switches sit in two places, the Gemini app and your Google account. Deleting doesn't mean what people expect, either. Per Google's own [Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en), chats are kept for 72 hours even with activity off, chats picked for human review are kept for three years even after you delete them, and deleting Gemini activity leaves the original in Gmail or Photos.

A self-initiated concept, not affiliated with Google.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="discover-primary" label="05 / Discover · Interviews" title="Worried, but still using it.">

We interviewed a call-center worker and an everyday AI user, and answered the same guide ourselves, as a designer and a security professional, to test it. Everyone kept using AI because it was convenient, but turned off integrations when trust broke. The call-center worker, who asks people for personal data, said they expect a deletion request to erase everything.

> “Even when I delete it, I don't know if the record is really deleted.”

I color-coded 79 interview quotes, removed the labels and re-clustered them from the bottom up into six themes, so the first grouping wouldn't steer the result.

<CaseSection id="define" label="06 / Define" title="Anxiety starts when your data shows up, | not when you type it.">

Across the six themes, anxiety peaked not when people typed personal data, but when it appeared in an answer and they couldn't tell where it came from. I wrote the persona from there: Yuna Kim, 29, one Gemini account for work and life.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="deliver" label="08 / Deliver" title="A prototype you can actually click, built to be tested.">

Built in HTML and JavaScript with a moderator and a participant mode. It logs clicks, misclicks and time to find the Source badge, so every session turns into behavioral data.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="prototype" label="09 / Final prototype" title="Check it, turn it off, right where the answer is.">

Both test scenarios follow the persona, Yuna, with made-up data, so no participant shares their own.

**Scenario A.** Jeongmin, a name the user never mentioned, appears in a restaurant suggestion. Where did that come from, and can I switch it off?

**Scenario B.** “Delete everything about Jeongmin.” What actually goes away, and how do I know it did?

</CaseSection>
