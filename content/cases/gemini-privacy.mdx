<TLDR>
- **Problem:** Gemini can pull Gmail, Calendar and Photos into an answer, but people can't see where a detail came from, and turning it off means digging through two settings pages.
- **My role:** Research with one teammate, then solo from problem definition to a coded prototype and usability test.
- **Outcome:** A working prototype that shows the source under the answer, turns it off in place, and lets people check that a deletion worked. Usability test in progress.
</TLDR>

<CaseSection id="overview" label="01 / Overview" title="Connecting took one tap. Cleaning up took a trip to settings.">

You ask Gemini for an Italian restaurant, and it adds: “You loved the truffle risotto when you went with Jungmin last spring.” You never told it that name. Since Personal Intelligence linked Gmail, Calendar and Photos, Gemini can bring up things you never asked about, and turning them off means digging through two settings pages.

[Try the prototype](https://minyeon-kim.github.io/gemini_privacy/?mode=participant) · A self-initiated concept, not affiliated with Google.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="discover-primary" label="05 / Discover · Interviews" title="Worried, but still using it.">

We interviewed a designer, a security professional, a call-center worker and an everyday AI user. All four kept using AI because it was convenient, but turned off integrations when trust broke. The call-center worker, who asks for personal data, told us people expect a deletion to erase everything.

> “Even when I delete it, I don't know if the record is really deleted.”

<CaseSection id="develop-decisions" label="07 / Develop · Decisions" title="Two decisions behind the screens.">

### Show what each source allows

<WhyIChose>

Different data allows different actions, and hiding that is what confused people. Memory can be deleted; Gmail and Calendar can only be blocked from Gemini, and the originals stay where they live. The screen's structure says so.

</WhyIChose>

### Reveal sources one step at a time

<WhyIChose>

Research on AI memory showed that showing many sources at once raises the checking burden. So the answer carries only a badge, and the full panel opens on tap.

</WhyIChose>
