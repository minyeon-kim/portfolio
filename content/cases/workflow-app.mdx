<TLDR>
- **Problem:** AI made designers and developers faster on their own, but merging what each built and agreeing on one version got slower, across Figma, GitHub and Slack.
- **My role:** Two-person team (Minyeon Kim, Dayeon Kim), research to testing done together: interviews, synthesis, an AI-built working prototype, a usability test and a co-design session.
- **Outcome:** A working prototype that compares and merges AI drafts and turns design–code gaps into requests, decisions and a history. Redesigned after testing; the second test is still ahead.
</TLDR>

<CaseSection id="overview" label="01 / Overview" title="AI made us faster alone — and slower together.">

We each built the same hotel booking site with AI and tried to merge the two. The same feature had different function names, and the AI had invented different rating rules: 4.2 stars in one, 4.3 in the other. Making things got faster; agreeing on one version got slower.

[Try the prototype](https://minyeon-kim.github.io/devsign/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/minyeon-kim/devsign)

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="discover" label="05 / Discover" title="Plenty of tools. None of them talk to each other.">

With almost no academic work, we started where practitioners write: 100+ posts from Reddit, Hacker News, the Figma forum and Korean communities. Each tool covers one step, but none connects spotting a difference to deciding what to do about it.

<CaseSection id="discover-primary" label="05 / Discover · Interviews" title="Making got faster. Checking, comparing and agreeing didn't.">

We interviewed a designer, a PM, a product designer and a developer, about 60 minutes each. We dropped questions that would apply without AI and asked who decides when design and build differ, and where the reason is kept.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="define" label="06 / Define" title="Decisions lived in chat, not in the work.">

We grouped 212 quotes bottom-up into 7 themes, then re-checked them in Dovetail. Shared context had the most cards (55), then tool fragmentation (38).

</CaseSection>

<CaseKey>What gets said never makes it back into the work, so <strong>no one can tell why it looks the way it does.</strong></CaseKey>

<CaseSection id="develop" label="07 / Develop" title="26 ideas, narrowed to the problem AI itself created.">

We scored 26 ideas on desirability, viability and feasibility. Our first pick, an AI mediator, looked novel but solved only one of our five problems. So we went back to the merge experiment: work made with AI didn't fit together, a problem that didn't exist before AI. We then built a working React prototype with AI and changed it 1,039 times in four weeks.

</CaseSection>

<CaseKey>We traded the novel idea for the real problem: <strong>work made with AI that won't merge.</strong></CaseKey>
